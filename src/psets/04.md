**(c) Coefficient of Determination**

When evaluating the performance of a regression model, we typically use the mean absolute error (MAE) rather than the mean squared error (MSE), as the MAE quantifies the difference between predicted and actual values in the same units as the target variable. Sometimes, however, to compare the performance of different models on different datasets, we want a metric that is bounded within a given range, e.g., between 0 and 1. This requirement is fulfilled by the coefficient of determination, which quantifies the proportion of variance in the target variable that is explained by the model:

$$
  R^2 = \frac{
    \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{N} (y_i - \bar{y}) (\hat{y}_i - \bar{y}) \right]^2
  }{
    \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{N} (y_i - \bar{y})^2 \right]
    \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{N} (\hat{y}_i - \bar{y})^2 \right]
  }\,,
$$

where $\bar{y}$ is the mean of the true target values, and $\hat{y}_i$ are the predicted values.

Implement the coefficient of determination and compute it for both the linear regression and the ridge regression models.
