Project Ideas

During the project phase, you will work in groups of 2–3 students and use the methods you have learned in this lecture to solve a real-world problem of your choice. The ideas below are starting points: you are welcome to use one of them as it is, adapt it to your interests, or propose a project idea of your own.

Visualising Newton fractals¶

The Newton–Raphson method does not only find roots: depending on the starting point, it can also create intricate convergence patterns. In this project, you will apply the Newton–Raphson method to a equations in the complex plane and visualise which root is reached from each starting point as a heatmap.

Related knowledge:

  • Newton–Raphson method (Sec. Root Finding)

  • heatmaps

Fitting absorbance data for the decay of tris(oxalato)manganate(III)¶

In the physical chemistry practical course, you used a linearised model of the absorbance decay of tris(oxalato)manganate(III) to determine the reaction rate constant. In this project, you will return to the original data and fit it directly with a more realistic nonlinear model, including a background correction.

Related knowledge:

  • numerical optimisation (Sec. Computational Optimisation)

  • nonlinear regression

Solving one-dimensional Schrödinger equations¶

Many quantum-mechanical model systems can be studied by solving the time-independent Schrödinger equation numerically. In this project, you will discretise a one-dimensional potential with the finite-difference method and compute the corresponding energy levels and wave functions.

Related knowledge:

  • eigenvalue algorithms (Sec. Eigenvalue Algorithms)

  • finite-difference method

Creating a spice map¶

Spices can be approximately represented as points in a high-dimensional vector space, where similarities between spices are encoded through scalar products or distances. In this project, you will use these distances to create a two-dimensional spice map, where similar spices appear close to each other and different spices appear farther apart.

Related knowledge:

  • singular value decomposition (Sec. Singular Value Decomposition)

  • principal coordinate analysis

Predicting TLC mobile phase compositions for organic molecules¶

Choosing a suitable mobile phase for thin-layer chromatography (TLC) is essential for successful separations, but it can be difficult without practical experience. In this project, you will curate a small dataset of organic molecules, train a simple regression model to predict suitable TLC mobile phase compositions, and evaluate its performance on a test set.

Related knowledge:

  • linear regression (Sec. Regression)

  • molecular representations

Classifying chemical samples from spectra¶

Spectra often contain enough information to distinguish between different types of chemical samples. In this project, you will train a simple classification model to assign spectra to predefined classes of molecules and evaluate how reliably the model recognises new samples.

Related knowledge:

  • classification

  • molecular representations
